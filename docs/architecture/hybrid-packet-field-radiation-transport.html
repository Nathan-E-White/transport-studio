Hybrid Packet–Field Radiation Transport

A goal-directed, quasi-steady algorithm that combines Gray M1, multigroup discrete ordinates, and continuous Monte Carlo defect correction, then projects one accepted radiation state into GRHD and BSSN.

Design in one page

One radiation state, three levels of fidelity

The method treats particle histories and deterministic fields as two evaluators of the same transport problem. A cheap field solve establishes the large-scale answer. Short, continuous Monte Carlo histories estimate where that answer is wrong. The accepted field—not the packets used to estimate it—is the radiation state coupled to matter and spacetime.

Level 0

Gray M1

Supplies a cheap moment estimate, an angular maximum-entropy prior, and a preconditioner. It does not remain as a separate radiation population after the hybrid solution is accepted.

Level 1

Discrete ordinates

Evolves a fixed spatial–angular–energy field with conservative face currents and groupwise collision redistribution.

Level 2

Monte Carlo defect

Uses continuous positions, directions, energies, and collision physics to estimate signed local errors in the coarse operator.

Coupling

One accepted tensor

Projects the corrected field into radiation stress-energy and matter four-force. Raw packet stress-energy is not added a second time.

Central modeling boundary. This first formulation is quasi-steady. The previous hybrid field is a warm start and spectral prior, not a conserved radiation census. Physical radiation memory requires a time-dependent transport mode such as IMC.
Physics and representation

From a continuous transport process to a finite field

Let a photon state be described by position x, direction Ω, energy E, and statistical source measure. A collision is governed by a transition kernel rather than a finite list of outcomes. A Monte Carlo history samples one path through that kernel; a deterministic method integrates a discretized version of all paths.

K(x,dx′)=k(x′|x)dx′ The continuous collision kernel assigns probability measure to outgoing phase-space states.

For a frozen, flat-space material state, the multigroup angular transport equation has the familiar form:

Ωm·∇ψmg +Σt,gψmg =qmg +∑m′,g′ Smg←m′g′ ψm′g′ Streaming plus total interaction equals external, emissive, and scattering sources.

Finite phase space and the Hilbert-space interpretation

The durable field lives in a finite approximation space spanned by spatial cells, angular basis functions, and global energy groups:

𝒱h=span{bi(x)am(Ω)eg(E)} Refinement enlarges the representable subspace: 𝒱h ⊂ 𝒱h′.

This is the practical Hilbert-space connection. The continuous transport solution is projected into a finite-dimensional function space with an inner product. Monte Carlo track estimators and deterministic coefficients become alternate estimators of projections onto the same basis. No quantum interference is implied.

Cell-volume response

U[i,m,g] stores integrated or averaged radiation response in one homogeneous transport cell, angular basis element, and energy group.

Oriented face current

J[f,m,g,±] stores incoming and outgoing angular-energy current. Internal faces provide a direct conservative balance between neighboring cells.

Fixed basis rules

  • Spatial transport cells are homogeneous with respect to the frozen material state used in one wave.
  • Angular projection uses nonnegative neighboring basis weights that form a partition of unity.
  • Energy groups are global and physics-aware, with boundaries at important source features, reaction thresholds, edges, and response discontinuities.
  • Exact MC energy is tallied into a group but is not replaced by the group-center energy during tracking.
  • Track length is an estimator coordinate, not a state coordinate, unless a future non-Markovian or time-of-flight model explicitly requires it.

Physical state, estimator ledger, and uncertainty

A phase-space cell should not contain one overloaded “weight.” It separates the accepted physical coefficient from Monte Carlo accumulators and sampling metadata.

Limg=∑swsℓs , LimgE=∑swsEsℓs Segment contributions are reduced by independent source history—not treated as independent observations.

For active GRHD coupling, source histories carry an absolute emission-rate measure. Track residence time ℓ/c converts a steady source rate into radiation occupancy; collision energy and momentum transfers become power and force densities.

Core algorithm

Frozen, versioned hybrid waves

Each wave is a bounded experiment against an immutable matter, geometry, source, boundary, basis, and sampling snapshot. The wave accumulates a correction privately, validates it, and either commits a new field atomically or changes nothing.

Freeze and fingerprint

Capture the compiled problem, matter primitives, metric, sources, boundaries, basis, accuracy contracts, resource limits, proposal policy, and random-number namespace.

Construct the level-zero prior

Lift Gray M1 moments into a nonnegative maximum-entropy angular distribution. Reuse the previous accepted spectrum when available; otherwise use a declared source or emissivity prior.

Solve the binned transport problem

Apply multigroup discrete ordinates with conservative finite-volume streaming, oriented face currents, and groupwise collision redistribution.

Estimate local continuous-physics defects

Pair exact and coarse flight/collision evaluations using common random variates. Collapse at collision or cell crossing and accumulate the signed difference by source history.

Apply a controlled correction

Solve the coarse equation with an MC-estimated source or closure defect. Choose damping through a positivity-preserving trust rule rather than clipping negative correction samples.

Refine where representation is inadequate

Between waves, split spatial, angular, or energy basis elements when response-weighted bias dominates uncertainty and pilot estimates reveal sub-bin structure.

Validate and publish

Check tally contracts, uncertainty, conservation, physical realizability, resource status, and a sealed full-history validation ensemble before atomically publishing the candidate.

Phase-space weight windows control splitting and roulette. Scalar cell importance is a user-facing shorthand; the executable window varies over cell, angular basis, energy group, and declared response. Interior zero-importance regions and arbitrary kill masks are prohibited.

Goal-directed accuracy and adjoint importance

Uniform accuracy over the entire phase-space field would recreate the dimensionality the method is intended to manage. Each tally declares absolute tolerance, relative tolerance, and confidence. Adjoint importance identifies upstream phase-space defects that influence those responses.

δJa≈⟨za,rMC⟩ The spatial location of a tally is not necessarily the location where transport uncertainty matters.

A biased proposal q may emphasize important paths, but it must retain support everywhere the physical kernel k permits transport. A safeguarded mixture provides exploration:

q=(1−ε)qgoal+εk,0<ε<1
Statistics and evidence

Reduce nearly everything; retain a defensible sample

The durable scientific record is a field, a set of estimator statistics, and a deliberately sampled audit—not an indiscriminate warehouse of particle tracks.

Aggregate evidence

Accepted coefficients, signed defects, face balances, sums and sums of squares by independent history, covariance needed by tallies, conservation residuals, and provenance.

Probability audit

Whole source histories selected before transport by a reproducible rule with known inclusion probability. Selected histories retain complete lineage and replay addresses.

For-cause evidence

Large weights, conservation failures, unusual interactions, user-designated regions, or other incidents. Diagnostic evidence is not used for population inference unless its selection probability is known.

Three separate measures

A retained contribution must not collapse physically distinct weights into one scalar:

wsource︸ dPdQ︸ Ehℓh︸ 1πh︸ Source measure × transport likelihood ratio × physical estimator contribution × audit inclusion correction.

Independent validation

A sealed ensemble of continuous end-to-end histories verifies final tallies but cannot guide defect correction, refinement, or importance maps. If a validation ensemble reveals a failure and is then used diagnostically, it is retired and replaced before the next acceptance decision.

Why the separation matters. Correction histories train the hybrid. Audit histories explain what it did. Validation histories judge whether the accepted answer is right. One history may be retained in more than one record, but those roles remain explicitly labeled.
Relativistic multiphysics

Quasi-steady radiation closure for GRHD and BSSN

The hybrid solve is performed against a frozen GRHD and spacetime snapshot. It returns a compact, validated coupling snapshot—not its internal histograms—to the relativistic kernel.

Eulerian bins, comoving collision physics

A photon carries exact future-directed null four-momentum while it is actively tracked. The durable field is binned in the local Eulerian orthonormal frame associated with the 3+1 foliation. Material interactions are evaluated in the fluid-comoving frame.

εEulerian=−pμnμ,εcomoving=−pμuμ The frame split makes Doppler, aberration, and gravitational redshift explicit.

Moment projection without an M1 pressure closure

The angle-resolved accepted field determines radiation energy density, flux, and pressure directly:

The coupling snapshot

The detailed phase-space field remains owned by the hybrid solver. GRHD and BSSN consume only:

  • cell-level radiation stress-energy Tradμν;
  • cell-level matter four-force Gμ from emission, absorption, and scattering;
  • uncertainty, conservation, realizability, and scale-separation evidence;
  • input-state, frame, basis, wave, and policy provenance.

Conservative exchange and gravity sourcing

∇μTmatterμν=Gν,∇μTradμν=−Gν

GRHD receives the accepted mean four-force only after its statistical uncertainty falls below a coupling-specific threshold. BSSN receives Tmatter + Trad. Collision deposition is evidence for Gμ, not an additional stress-energy tensor.

Shared mesh and conservative restriction

The GRHD grid is the base coupling mesh. Transport may refine beneath one GRHD cell, but may not cross its boundary. Subcell four-force is conservatively restricted:

GIμ=1VI∑i⊂IViGiμ

Quasi-steady validity

Active coupling is permitted only when radiation relaxes materially faster than the hydrodynamic state changes. A practical policy compares light-crossing, interaction, and diffusion times with a fluid-evolution timescale.

tradthydro≤η,η≪1

When scale separation fails, the steady solution may still run as a passive diagnostic. It cannot backreact. The required extension is time-dependent radiation transport, not a more determined steady solve.

Verification strategy

A ladder that isolates failure causes

  1. Transport-only homogeneous slab. Flat spacetime, stationary material, fixed source, pure absorption followed by isotropic scattering. Verify analytic attenuation, leakage, deposition, and deterministic/MC agreement.
  2. Passive GRHD projection. Freeze stationary Valencia matter and compute Tradμν and Gμ. Verify units, frames, realizability, absolute source normalization, and stress-energy projection.
  3. Active stationary exchange. Apply equal-and-opposite four-force while geometry remains fixed. Verify primitive recovery, conservation, and timestep convergence.
  4. Moving homogeneous fluid. Introduce a known velocity and test Doppler shift, aberration, and Eulerian/comoving balance.
  5. Radiative shock tube. Exercise spatially varying GRHD states, stiff source exchange, and the existing shock verification substrate.
  6. Curved-spacetime passive transport. Verify null geodesic redshift, angular remapping, and tensor projection without geometry backreaction.
  7. Active BSSN sourcing. Advance with the combined matter-plus-radiation tensor and monitor Hamiltonian, momentum, algebraic, and gauge constraints.
First tracer bullet. Stop at level 2: a flat, stationary, homogeneous slab whose accepted hybrid field produces a validated passive GRHD coupling snapshot. This proves the new boundary before moving-matter exchange or BSSN evolution is asked to certify it.
Practical assessment

What the method buys—and what it does not

Advantages

  • Bounds durable state through phase-space aggregation.
  • Uses deterministic work for bulk transport and MC where continuous physics matters.
  • Targets declared responses rather than promising uniform high-dimensional accuracy.
  • Produces energy, momentum, face-current, uncertainty, and provenance evidence.
  • Computes angular pressure directly rather than relying on M1 closure after refinement.
  • Supports conservative, frame-explicit GRHD and BSSN coupling.

Costs and failure modes

  • Signed stochastic defects complicate positivity and convergence.
  • Fine/coarse pairs lose correlation if local synchronization is poorly designed.
  • Angular, energy, spatial, statistical, and coupling errors must be distinguished.
  • Relativistic frame remapping introduces energy-space and angular-space transport.
  • Quasi-steady coupling is invalid for radiation fronts or slow radiation relaxation.
  • Independent validation and audit evidence consume nontrivial compute and storage.

The concept is internally consistent, but it is not automatically faster than either a mature deterministic code or a mature Monte Carlo code. Its advantage depends on correlated fine/coarse corrections, a useful low-fidelity model, sparse goal importance, and enough repeated solves for the hierarchy to amortize its machinery.

Canonical language

Glossary

Accepted radiation field
The single published phase-space response field after deterministic solution, MC correction, and acceptance gates.
Accuracy contract
Per-response absolute tolerance, relative tolerance, and confidence requirement.
Audit history
A whole source history retained under a known, outcome-independent inclusion probability for reconstruction and statistical auditing.
Coupling snapshot
A compact cell-level projection containing radiation stress-energy, matter four-force, uncertainty, conservation evidence, and provenance.
Defect field
A signed MC estimate of where the coarse transport equation or closure is wrong. It is evidence, not physical energy.
Goal importance
Adjoint sensitivity of a declared response to a local phase-space source or defect.
Hybrid packet–field transport
A method in which deterministic fields and MC packets evaluate one transport model at different levels of fidelity.
Material-homogeneous transport cell
A cell using one frozen reconstructed matter state and one associated set of transport coefficients during a wave.
Quasi-steady coupling
A coupling approximation in which radiation is solved to steady state against frozen matter and geometry before one physical GRHD/BSSN step.
Validation ensemble
A sealed set of full continuous histories that judges accepted results but does not guide the algorithm that produced them.
Wave
An immutable-input, private-accumulation, validate-then-commit cycle of deterministic and MC work.
Weight window
A phase-space range controlling variance splitting and expectation-preserving roulette.
